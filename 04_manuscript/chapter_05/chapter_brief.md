## Chapter Purpose

This is the first true business-line chapter in Part II. Its job is to explain asset servicing as a durable but operationally burdened business. The chapter should show what custody and fund accounting actually do, how the business makes money, why clients depend on it, and where the current operating model still relies on fragmented systems, manual work, reconciliations, and exception handling beneath a polished external surface.

The chapter should make the reader see asset servicing as both a highly valuable infrastructure business and an unusually fertile target for AI-native redesign later in the book. It should not sound like a product brochure or a generic consulting overview. It should sound like someone who understands how the business really runs.

## Key Reader Questions

- What does asset servicing actually include in practice?
- How do custody, safekeeping, fund accounting, reporting, and investor servicing fit together?
- Why is this business stable and valuable despite being operationally messy underneath?
- Where do manual complexity, fragmented data, and exception-heavy processes still dominate?
- Why does this business line matter as a candidate for later transformation?

## Scope

### In scope

- custody and safekeeping
- fund accounting and books-and-records workflows
- reporting, investor servicing, and operational oversight
- business model and revenue logic at a high level
- workflow pain points, exception handling, reconciliations, and fragmentation

### Out of scope

- full cross-business synthesis, which belongs more in CH11
- future-state AI redesign, which belongs in CH19
- detailed clearing and settlement mechanics reserved for CH06
- broad platform thesis language reserved for Part III

## Desired Reader Outcome

The reader should conclude that asset servicing is a stable, high-trust, high-complexity business whose current operating model still depends on heavy coordination work. The chapter should leave the reader believing two things at once:

1. this is a strong current business
2. this business is more operationally brittle and labor-intensive than the polished client surface suggests

## Structural Guidance

Suggested sequence:

1. Define what asset servicing covers and why it matters.
2. Explain the business model and why clients pay for these services.
3. Walk through the workflow reality: custody, accounting, reporting, servicing, oversight.
4. Surface where the model breaks: fragmented systems, reconciliations, data quality, exceptions, manual review.
5. Close by establishing why this is a strong candidate for AI-native reinvention later.
