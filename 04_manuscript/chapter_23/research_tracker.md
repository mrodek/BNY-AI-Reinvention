## Working Hypotheses

- CH23 will be strongest if it argues that AI makes analytical support abundant faster than it makes fiduciary judgment unnecessary.
- Durable edge shifts away from raw research throughput alone and toward mandate interpretation, governance quality, process credibility, institutional memory, and trusted client explanation.
- The chapter should describe AI-native investment management as a continuously assisted investment process, not as a fully autonomous portfolio machine.
- BNY's direct edge in this chapter is likely clearer in its combination of specialist-manager judgment and platform-heavy supporting infrastructure than in any single public claim about front-office AI.

## Source Selection Notes

- The kickoff source set is deliberately small and curated. It combines direct BNY evidence on investment management and the supporting investment-data stack (`SRC-001` to `SRC-004`) with practitioner and industry sources on workflow change, governance, and economics (`SRC-005` to `SRC-007`).
- Official BNY sources were prioritized because CH23 needs to remain plausibly BNY-shaped rather than reading like a generic asset-management future essay.
- CFA Institute was prioritized over broader media commentary because it better distinguishes where AI supports the investment process versus where practitioner judgment still matters.
- McKinsey is included to make the economics and operating-model pressure visible, especially margin compression, fragmented technology stacks, and the need for domain-level redesign.
- Intentionally excluded generic "AI stock picker" commentary, sensational market-prediction claims, and vendor marketing about autonomous portfolio management because they would push the chapter toward hype instead of disciplined operating design.

## Themes Emerging

- Investment management may become one of the clearest cases where analytical leverage expands faster than trust and accountability can be delegated.
- The future-state operating model likely centers on research synthesis, portfolio diagnostics, implementation support, committee memory, and client explanation rather than on unsupervised investing.
- The harder competitive question is not who has AI tools, but who turns AI abundance into a better-governed and more trusted investment process.

## Evidence Gaps

- Public BNY material is stronger on specialist-manager positioning and the surrounding investment-data stack than on explicit AI use inside front-office investment decisions.
- One more source on fee pressure or commoditization may help sharpen the closing argument if CH23 leans heavily into economics.
