## Research Passes

### Pass 1 - Durability Through Trusted Scale And Embedded Breadth

- `SRC-001` is the strongest opening-section source so far for CH04. It explicitly states that clients are consolidating providers to reduce operational risk, improve resiliency, and seek fewer handoffs, clearer accountability, better data, and faster cycle times. That is direct support for the chapter's durability logic rather than just a generic corporate growth claim.
- `SRC-001` also supports the argument that BNY's breadth is hard to replicate. The annual report says BNY offers differentiated end-to-end offerings that are highly valuable for clients and difficult to replicate, and notes that multi-business client adoption has grown materially. This supports embeddedness, adjacency, and switching-cost logic.
- `SRC-002` and `SRC-005` reinforce the same point from a solutions perspective: BNY presents itself as helping clients across every stage of the financial lifecycle, combining expertise, platforms, data, analytics, and operations. This is useful support for the chapter's claim that the moat comes from being wired into multiple handoffs rather than one product alone.
- `SRC-004` is useful for grounding the trust argument in plain language. Custody is not just possession; it includes settlement, safekeeping, and reporting. That makes it a continuity-sensitive, trust-sensitive function, which is central to the chapter's moat argument.
- `SRC-006` and `SRC-007` provide external comparative support for the broader infrastructure logic. DTCC's resilience framing emphasizes continuity of critical services, risk reduction, standardization, and secure operation. While DTCC is not BNY, the same market-infrastructure logic helps justify why resilience and low failure tolerance create durable positions.
- `SRC-003` remains useful but should be handled carefully. GSIB status supports importance and supervisory relevance, but by itself it does not prove switching costs or commercial durability. It should support the regulatory-relevance layer, not carry the whole chapter.

## Working Hypotheses

- BNY's moat is strongest where trust, records, continuity, and operational integration matter more than consumer preference.
- Embedded infrastructure relationships create switching costs that are operational and governance-heavy, not just contractual.
- Breadth across lifecycle functions makes BNY more durable than a narrow single-product provider.
- Regulation should be framed as both constraint and moat: it raises cost and scrutiny, but it also reinforces the premium on trusted scale and continuity.

## Source Selection Notes

- Prioritize `SRC-001`, `SRC-002`, `SRC-004`, and `SRC-008` for the first draft because they support durability with relatively plain language.
- Use `SRC-006` and `SRC-007` as comparative infrastructure support rather than as direct evidence about BNY specifically.
- Keep `SRC-003` in reserve for systemic-importance context and regulatory relevance, but do not let GSIB status become a substitute for the more commercial durability argument.

## Gaps

- Need stronger source support for switching-cost logic beyond BNY's own trusted-provider framing.
- Need one or two clean examples of why replacement or migration risk is unusually high in infrastructure businesses.
- Need evidence on how regulation and trust reinforce durability without overstating the point.
