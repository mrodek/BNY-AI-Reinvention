## Source Selection Notes

- Why these sources were chosen: this first pass prioritizes primary BNY materials for role and scale, Federal Reserve sources for systemic and infrastructure context, and two large peer references to calibrate BNY's position against obvious comparison points
- What was intentionally excluded: generic AI-transformation material, retail-banking commentary, unsourced think pieces, and competitor commentary that did not clearly address custody, servicing, or financial infrastructure
- What still needs to be added: clearer source material on asset servicing specifically, sharper evidence on BNY's place among custody and infrastructure peers, and at least one source or example that makes the invisible-infrastructure role vivid in plain English

## Themes Emerging

- Theme 1: BNY's importance comes from its embedded role in market operations rather than public visibility
- Theme 2: BNY can be framed credibly as one of the most important infrastructure institutions in finance without immediately jumping to absolutist claims
- Theme 3: The opening chapter should contrast visible banks with hidden financial infrastructure providers before it explains market plumbing in more depth
- Theme 4: Market infrastructure is best explained through the finality of settlement and the interdependence of systems rather than through abstract "back office" language alone

## Gaps

- Gap 1: still need a stronger plain-English explainer for asset servicing specifically, though custody, clearing, and settlement support is now materially better
- Gap 2: still need a concrete example or vignette that makes BNY's invisible role legible to a non-specialist reader
- Gap 3: still need sharper sourcing on BNY's precise ranking and comparative role versus specific peers in each business line
