## Working Hypotheses

- Investment management is best understood as two connected businesses: differentiated investment judgment and the operating stack that supports, implements, measures, and governs that judgment.
- AI pressure is stronger and easier to see in the operating and analytical layers than in the narrow core of differentiated human judgment.
- The chapter will be strongest if it avoids arguing that AI simply replaces portfolio managers and instead explains how AI changes the shape of advantage.

## Source Selection Notes

- Reviewed sources now cover three distinct needs: BNY's direct investment-management positioning (`SRC-001`), BNY's surrounding data/accounting/lifecycle platform role (`SRC-002` to `SRC-004`), and independent views on AI's workflow and governance effects in asset management (`SRC-005` to `SRC-009`).
- Chosen independent sources prioritize CFA Institute material because it is closest to practitioner reality and better suited than generic AI commentary for distinguishing where models help versus where human investment judgment still matters.
- McKinsey is included to make economics, margin pressure, and fragmented technology stacks visible at the industry level, but it should remain a supporting source rather than the chapter's sole strategic foundation.
- Intentionally excluded generic media commentary on "AI stock picking," sensational claims about full manager replacement, and broad market-prediction futurism because the chapter needs workflow and business-model specificity, not spectacle.
- Remaining evidence gap: one more neutral source on fee or product commoditization could sharpen the chapter later, but the current source set is sufficient for `draft_v1`.

## Themes Emerging

- Investment management combines discretionary or model-driven judgment with a large supporting machine of data, controls, reporting, and implementation.
- AI changes more than just research productivity; it also changes data handling, workflow speed, compliance burden, and the cost of analysis.
- The strategic tension is not "human versus machine." It is what remains differentiated once more of the surrounding process becomes cheaper and more scalable.

## Pass 1 Findings

- BNY's role in this chapter is twofold. Through BNY Investments, the firm is directly in the business of managing assets through specialist investment managers. Through BNY's broader data, accounting, and lifecycle platforms, it also sits underneath investment organizations as an operating and information layer.
- The chapter should not treat investment management as only a front-office craft business. The official BNY material makes the supporting machine visible: data normalization, accounting books of record, performance analytics, audit trails, reporting, reconciliations, and lifecycle support all sit underneath portfolio decisions.
- Independent sources support the claim that AI is entering many parts of the investment process already, including research synthesis, portfolio construction support, trade execution efficiency, data analysis, compliance monitoring, and operational workflows.
- The strongest disciplined claim is not that AI replaces portfolio managers outright. It is that AI increases the amount of analysis, process, and control work that can be scaled, automated, or compressed, which in turn forces a sharper distinction between durable judgment and commoditizable process.
- Governance and trust remain central. CFA Institute material repeatedly emphasizes transparency, accountability, and human oversight in high-stakes financial decisions, which matters because investment management cannot simply optimize for automation without preserving fiduciary trust and explainability.
- The economics bridge is supportable. McKinsey gives a strong current industry frame around margin pressure, fragmented systems, heavy run-the-business spending, and the fact that AI value is most meaningful when firms move beyond isolated use cases to domain-level workflow redesign.

## Gaps

- One more neutral source on fee or product commoditization could still sharpen the economics section later if needed.
- The chapter still needs a deliberate choice about whether later revisions should emphasize asset managers, asset owners, or both as the primary operating lens.
- BNY-specific front-office detail remains lighter than the evidence available for BNY's data, accounting, and operating platform roles, so the chapter should avoid overclaiming direct investment-process specificity beyond what the public material supports.
