## Drafting Notes

- `draft_v1` uses official BNY and Pershing sources as the main external evidence base. This keeps the platform argument grounded in BNY's actual public operating scope rather than generic technology-platform theory.
- The chapter uses `SRC-008` as a manuscript bridge rather than as an external citation. CH12 inherits CH11's diagnosis that the current system is held together by reconciliation and exception management.
- The phrase "financial operating system" is used as a strategic and organizational metaphor. The draft should not imply that BNY currently runs every business on one literal unified software operating system.
- Source markers are intentionally light. Full citation detail belongs here, while the chapter prose should stay executive-readable.

## Claim Support Detail

### BNY As Shared Operating Infrastructure

`SRC-001` supports the scale and broad platform framing. `SRC-002`, `SRC-003`, `SRC-004`, `SRC-005`, `SRC-006`, and `SRC-007` collectively support the argument that BNY operates across records, data, investment lifecycle support, cash movement, collateral, wealth workflows, and market-facing services.

### Operating-System Layer Map

The layer map is a synthesis from official product and platform pages:

- State and record layer: custody, fund accounting, investment accounting, data management, and portfolio records supported by `SRC-002`, `SRC-003`, and `SRC-007`.
- Movement layer: payments, cash management, trade finance, FX payments, and transaction banking network supported by `SRC-004`.
- Resource-allocation layer: collateral mobility, optimization, liquidity ecosystem, APIs, real-time data, and securities-financing support from `SRC-005`.
- Workflow layer: Pershing clearing, custody, advisory, wealth technology, financing, and institutional services from `SRC-006`.
- Control and reporting layer: data quality, reconciliations, transparency, dashboards, reporting, risk management, and open architecture from `SRC-003`, `SRC-004`, and `SRC-007`.

### Platform Economics

The platform-economics argument is treated as strategic interpretation grounded in observable dependency. BNY's public materials repeatedly emphasize integration, open architecture, scale, data, workflows, networks, and operating leverage. The chapter should avoid claiming that these forces guarantee future advantage. They create a strategic base that becomes more valuable if BNY can add a unified intelligence layer.

### Bridge To CH13

`SRC-003` and `SRC-007` already use intelligence-adjacent language around data, analytics, actionable insights, and total portfolio view. `SRC-008` shows why that is not enough by itself: the existing system still requires reconciliation and exception handling across fragmented records and statuses. CH13 should therefore define the missing intelligence layer as an orchestration and meaning layer above the existing operating platform.
