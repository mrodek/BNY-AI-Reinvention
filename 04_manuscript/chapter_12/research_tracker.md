## Working Hypotheses

- CH12 should argue that BNY is already platform-like because other institutions depend on it for shared financial state, movement, records, liquidity, collateral, and workflow execution.
- The platform-economics material from CH13 should be compact and tied to BNY's actual operating dependencies rather than discussed as generic theory.
- The chapter should end by showing that the current platform still lacks the intelligence layer needed to reduce reconciliation burden and orchestrate cross-product decisions.

## Source Selection Notes

- The initial source set prioritizes official BNY sources because the chapter's platform claim must be anchored in the firm's actual operating scope, not just in abstract strategy theory.
- One external platform-strategy source is queued as optional, but the chapter should not rely heavily on generic platform language unless the BNY-specific evidence is insufficient.
- Internal CH11 source notes are included as manuscript context because CH12 must directly inherit the reconciliation diagnosis.
- Current evidence gap: need to decide whether the operating-system layer map can be built entirely from prior chapter evidence plus BNY sources, or whether a small amount of external platform theory improves reader confidence.

## Themes Emerging

- The strongest CH12 argument is not "BNY could become a platform." It is "BNY already behaves like one in several core businesses."
- Platform economics are more persuasive when described through operating dependency than through generic network-effect terminology.
- The clean bridge to CH13 is that platform breadth alone does not create intelligence. It creates the surface area on which intelligence could operate.
- BNY's own public language supports a disciplined layer map: investment lifecycle, data and analytics, global payments and trade, collateral, Pershing wealth workflows, and integrated open architecture.
- The chapter should use "financial operating system" as a reader-facing organizing model, not as a literal software claim.

## Research Pass 1 Findings

- Official BNY sources are sufficient to support `draft_v1` without importing generic platform theory. BNY's own materials repeatedly use platform language and show breadth across securities services, data, payments, collateral, Pershing, and investment lifecycle workflows.
- The best evidence is not any single claim that BNY is an "operating system." The evidence is the pattern across sources: clients depend on BNY for records, workflows, movement of money, mobility of assets, data, risk controls, and operating leverage.
- The operating-system metaphor should be written as a disciplined map of layers: state, movement, resource allocation, workflow, data, control, and intelligence-adjacent surfaces.
- The CH13 material should be fully absorbed into one compact section on platform economics. The chapter should explain trust, embedded workflows, switching cost, data gravity, and operating leverage through financial-infrastructure dependency rather than through software-company analogies.
- The bridge to CH13 should be sober: BNY has many platform ingredients, but breadth does not automatically create a unified intelligence layer. The next chapter should define that missing layer.

## Source Selection Rationale

- Included sources were selected because they are direct BNY or Pershing materials that describe operating scope, platform capabilities, data, payments, collateral, and workflow surfaces. For CH12, primary company sources are more useful than broad industry commentary because the argument is about BNY's actual strategic shape.
- The CH11 source notes were included as internal manuscript evidence because CH12 must inherit the reconciliation diagnosis rather than restating the entire Part II source base.
- External platform strategy theory was deferred. It may be useful later for a short supporting note, but it is not necessary for `draft_v1` and could weaken the chapter by making a BNY-specific platform argument sound generic.
- Evidence gap retained: public sources can support the strategic reframing, but they cannot prove the internal integration depth of every platform layer. The draft therefore treats "financial operating system" as a strategic interpretation grounded in observable operating scope, not as a claim that all BNY businesses currently operate on one unified technical substrate.
