## Purpose

- What this chapter must accomplish: give the reader a clean mental model of how capital moves through the system, explain where BNY sits in that flow, and prepare the ground for the more detailed lifecycle walkthrough in CH03

## Reader

- Primary reader persona: senior executive or strategy-minded reader who understands business systems well but does not already understand financial market infrastructure
- Secondary reader persona: operator, architect, or investor-minded reader testing whether the book truly understands the machine beneath finance

## Key Questions

- How does capital actually move through the financial system after an investment decision is made?
- How do custody, clearing, settlement, liquidity, and servicing fit together as one machine rather than as disconnected products?
- Where does BNY sit inside that broader machine?
- Why is understanding this flow necessary before talking about platform strategy or AI reinvention?

## Core Claims

- Claim 1: Financial infrastructure is best understood as a connected asset machine rather than as isolated institutional product lines.
- Claim 2: BNY's strategic significance becomes much clearer when the reader sees how many points in the asset lifecycle depend on infrastructure coordination.
- Claim 3: The firm's opportunity to become an intelligent platform depends on already being embedded across the lifecycle of assets, cash, servicing, and reporting.

## Evidence Needed

- Literature or standards needed: sources on post-trade market structure, custody, clearing, settlement, servicing, liquidity, and the asset lifecycle
- Practitioner examples needed: one or two examples that make the movement of assets and cash tangible
- Internal stories or lessons needed: lived explanations of where coordination failures or handoffs typically create friction

## Structure

1. Start with the idea that most people see financial institutions as separate firms rather than one connected machine.
2. Explain the major stages in the asset lifecycle at a high level.
3. Show how custody, clearing, settlement, liquidity, and servicing connect.
4. Place BNY inside that flow without yet turning the chapter into a company profile.
5. Explain why fragmented understanding causes people to miss where power and leverage actually sit.
6. Bridge into CH03, which will make the lifecycle even more concrete through one trade.
