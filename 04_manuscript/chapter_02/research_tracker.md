## Source Selection Notes

- Why these sources were chosen: this first CH02 pass prioritizes authoritative lifecycle explainers plus BNY primary sources that map the firm across custody, fund accounting, payments, clearing, financing, and collateral; together they support a longer chapter that explains the machine through flows rather than through isolated business names
- What was intentionally excluded: generic AI material, broad bank strategy commentary, and highly technical market-structure sources that would make the chapter harder for a non-specialist to absorb at this stage
- What still needs to be added: a sharper independent source on where BNY sits across the post-trade and servicing stack, and possibly a simpler lifecycle diagram source or terminology aid

## Themes Emerging

- Theme 1: the chapter should teach the reader to see finance as a connected machine
- Theme 2: BNY's role becomes more obvious when the machine is described through flows rather than institutions
- Theme 3: the strongest framing is not simply that BNY participates in many businesses, but that it sits across multiple linked stages of the investment lifecycle
- Theme 4: the lifecycle argument gets stronger when liquidity, collateral, servicing, and reporting are treated as connected to settlement rather than as separate add-ons
- Theme 5: asset servicing is not just back-office administration; it is a connected operating layer spanning accounting, administration, investor servicing, reporting, and data

## Gaps

- Gap 1: still need a simple, memorable lifecycle model that can structure the chapter prose
- Gap 2: still need one or two examples that make the movement from transaction to servicing to reporting feel concrete
- Gap 3: still need to decide how much of BNY's business taxonomy belongs in this chapter versus later business-line chapters
